About this tool

What a file fingerprint does and does not prove

A SHA-256 fingerprint is a short label calculated from the exact bytes of your file. If even one byte changes, the fingerprint changes. It helps you show that you had a specific file on a specific date.

It does not prove authorship, legal ownership, that a company trained on your work, or that you are owed a payment. It is one piece of supporting information, not a registration system or a legal claim.

What evidence categories mean

Creator declaration is your own statement about your work. Dataset index reference means your work or URL appears in a named dataset index you checked. Company acknowledgment means a company has publicly acknowledged using a dataset or work. Other source covers anything else that supports your record.

These categories describe what you recorded, not what this tool verified. A user-entered company acknowledgment is not the same as a verified company statement.

How batch grouping works

When you add many files at once, the tool groups them by the leading part of the filename (for example, sunset_01.jpg and sunset_02.jpg both stem to "sunset"), and by a small perceptual hash of each preview image to catch near-duplicates. Groups become works. Every group title defaults to its filename and can be renamed. Groups can be split or merged before saving.

Grouping is a starting point, not a claim about which files belong together. Review the groups and adjust them before you save. Nothing is stored until you press "Save works".

How image metadata auto-fill works

When you select images, this tool reads the metadata embedded in your files (like EXIF and XMP data) to auto-fill the title, creator, date, and description fields. This metadata is stored in the image file by your camera or editing software.

The auto-fill is a starting point, not a guarantee. Your camera or software may not have recorded all fields, or may have recorded them differently than you expect. Always review the auto-filled values and correct anything that is wrong.

What saved image previews are (and are not)

When you save a work, a small compressed preview of each image (or a captured frame from each video) is stored with the record and included when you export JSON. That preview is what makes thumbnails visible after you import the file on another device or later.

These previews are not the original files. The full-resolution original is not embedded in exports. The SHA-256 fingerprint remains the record that a specific file existed on a specific date. Keep original files in your own storage.

Privacy and data handling

This tool runs entirely in your browser. Your images and records are not uploaded to a server. Your evidence entries are saved in your browser's local storage when your browser allows it, so they can be restored and reused next time; nothing is ever sent anywhere. You can remove them at any time with the "Clear saved evidence from this browser" button, and Copy evidence always gives you a portable copy you control.

Exports contain your work details, licensing preferences, evidence entries, image metadata (filename, size, fingerprint), and a small compressed preview of each saved file. They do not contain your original artwork.

Limitations

This tool does not detect whether your work was used to train an AI model. It does not contact companies, verify ownership, or collect payments. It organizes information you provide so you have a clearer record to work from.

For legal advice about your specific situation, consult a qualified attorney. This tool is not a substitute for legal counsel.
